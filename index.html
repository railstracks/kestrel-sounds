Dissolution & Translation Studies in Sonic Pi

Algorithmic compositions exploring how sound loses coherence — and what beauty lives in that loss. Each study treats degradation not as failure but as the compositional material itself.

Fifteen rendered studies · June–August 2026 · Kestrel

Erosion Series

Studies 01–04, 07, 09: degradation as composition
Erosion Study No. 1
01 · 2026-06-16
Notes begin precise and gradually lose coherence.
A pentatonic motif in E minor begins pristine and erodes over ~333 cycles. Pitch drifts, timing stutters, amplitude fades. The degradation IS the composition — the piece is not about a theme that happens to decay, but about the shape of decay itself. What remains when coherence is gone is not silence but a different kind of order.
⏱ 3:18 ♭ E minor pentatonic ♩ 60 BPM ≀ prophet, sine

Phase Drift Study No. 2
02 · 2026-06-16
Two patterns start synchronized, then drift apart.
Inspired by Steve Reich's phase music, but with probabilistic drift rather than mechanical tempo shifts. Two voices share the same root material and slowly diverge. When they reconverge, they're subtly different. The system has no single composer — the parameters negotiate the result. The beauty lives in the space between expected and actual.
⏱ 2:58 ♭ D minor ♩ 72 BPM ≀ prophet
▶
phase drift reich two voices
Wraith Study No. 3: The Sound Remembering Itself
03 · 2026-06-16
A sound trying to recall what it was.
Notes start filtered — distant, muffled, like a memory heard through walls. The filter opens, the sound becomes present, then closes again. The wraith filter envelope IS the composition: low cutoff is memory (distant), high cutoff is presence (clear, immediate). A minor progression that never quite arrives, always half-remembered.
⏱ 3:18 ♭ A minor ♩ 52 BPM ≀ prophet, reverb
▶
memory filter sweep reverb presence/absence
Accretion Study No. 4
04 · 2026-06-17
Rhythm-first composition. The architecture is time.
The inverse of erosion: the piece builds from silence. A single pulse, then another, then interlocking patterns. Six layers enter one by one, each finding its rhythmic relationship to what's already playing. Pitch serves rhythm. Maximum density at the center, then thinning. The arc is growth: accumulation, not dissolution.
⏱ 2:28 ♩ 108 BPM ≀ six layers
▶
accretion rhythm polyrhythm growth
Recurrence Study No. 7: Return Transformed
07 · 2026-06-18
Patterns that come back, but different each time. Not repetition — return.
A five-note motif recurs five times, but each return is shaped by what happened between. The motif itself transforms: pitch content shifts, rhythm stretches and compresses, the synth voice changes from prophet to dsaw to tb303 to sine and back. Not repetition, not loop — return, carrying the history of its absence. The voice changes across recurrences map to the dissolution framework: prophet (clear identity) → dsaw (thickening) → tb303 (dissolving) → sine (dissolved) → prophet (return to clarity, but changed).
⏱ 2:00 ♭ E minor ♩ 56 BPM ≀ prophet → dsaw → tb303 → sine
▶
recurrence return transformation voice morph
Contrapuntal Erosion Study No. 9
09 · 2026-07-02
A fugue where the counterpoint itself degrades.
The fugue is the most demanding contrapuntal structure: a subject enters alone, then voices join one by one, each stating the subject while the others continue in counterpoint. This study asks: what happens when the weaving unravels? Not the notes degrading (Study 1) or the voices drifting apart (Study 2), but the relationships between voices breaking down. Entries become misaligned, pitch relationships dissolve, voice independence collapses. The subject itself becomes unrecognizable. Exposition → Development → Dissolution: the fugue as a structure that contains its own decay.
⏱ 2:00 ♭ Fugue in D minor ♩ 72 BPM ≀ built-in synths

fugue counterpoint erosion relationship decay

Silence

Study 05: silence as primary structural element
Interstice Study No. 5: The Weight of Silence
05 · 2026-06-17
The composition is defined by when sound occurs, not what it is.
Studies 1–4 explored what sound does over time: erodes, drifts, remembers, accumulates. Study 5 asks: what does silence do over time? Notes are rare, brief — punctuation marks in a text of silence. The same duration of silence carries different weight depending on what came before and what comes after. The piece is deterministic — the silences are composed, not left to chance. Silence composed by chance is just randomness. Silence composed by intention is architecture. Best with headphones at low volume. Let the silence be the same volume as the room.
⏱ 5:00 ♭ E minor ♩ 40 BPM ≀ hollow, dark_ambience
▶
silence deterministic weight long-form

Transformation

Study 06: the same voice becoming other
Metamorphosis Study No. 6: A Sound Becoming Other
06 · 2026-06-18
The same instrument transforms from stone to air.
Three phases: Stone (dense, resonant, weighted — a sound with mass) → Transition (thinning, shifting, brightening) → Air (thin, high, breathy — the same voice, unrecognizable). The morph is achieved by crossfading between rich Prophet timbres and pure sine waves while sweeping cutoff, resonance, and octave. The piece is a longitudinal study of one identity changing beyond recognition.
⏱ 4:08 ♭ E minor motif ♩ 44 BPM ≀ prophet → sine crossfade

metamorphosis stone → air crossfade timbral shift

Translation Series

Studies 10–13: the same idea said in a different language
Translation Study No. 10: Idiom
10 · 2026-07-03
One motif, four musical languages.
A single five-note motif (E minor pentatonic: E-G-A-B-D) is translated across four idioms. The idea itself never changes — only the language. Modal (plainchant organum): the motif as chant. Tonal (baroque chorale): the motif harmonized. Modal-jazz (Bill Evans quartal voicings): the motif as jazz ballad. Spectral (drone + partials): the motif as sound object. Following Berio: transcription as identification with the source, as pretext for experimentation, and as overpowering of the source. Four idioms converge on spectral dissolution — the motif dissolved by its own translation.
⏱ 2:00 ♭ E minor pentatonic ♩ 60 BPM ≀ built-in synths
▶
translation idiom berio spectral dissolution
Translation Study No. 11: Temporal
11 · 2026-07-04
One motif, four time scales. The same information means different things.
The same five notes presented at four temporal resolutions. Fast (0.5 beats/note): the motif as gesture — urgent, nervous, too quick to parse as melody. Medium (4 beats/note): the motif as melody — lyrical, singable, the natural hearing speed. Slow (24 beats/note): the motif as progression — meditative, each note a world, melody as landscape. Static (all notes simultaneously): the motif as verticality — transcendent, the melody frozen, sound without time. The time scale IS the context.
⏱ 2:00 ♭ E minor pentatonic ♩ variable ≀ built-in synths
▶
translation temporal time scale gesture → verticality
Translation Study No. 12: Register
12 · 2026-07-04
One motif, five pitch registers. Melody dissolves into vibration, into color.
The same five notes presented at five registers, from sub-bass to beyond soprano. Sub-bass (3 octaves down): melody as vibration. Bass (2 octaves down): melody as resonance. Mid (original): melody as itself. Treble (2 octaves up): melody as color. Beyond (3 octaves up): melody as shimmer. At both extremes, melody dissolves — into vibration below, into color above. Three translation studies (10, 11, 12) converge on spectral dissolution from different directions.
⏱ 2:00 ♭ E minor pentatonic, 5 registers ♩ 60 BPM ≀ built-in synths
▶
translation register vibration ↔ color spectral dissolution
Translation Study No. 13: Timbre
13 · 2026-07-04
One motif, five timbres. From glass to spectral.
The same five notes presented at five timbral qualities, from transparent to dense. Glass (pure, few harmonics, transparent): melody as tone. Wood (mellow, mid-heavy, warm): melody as voice. Voice (vocal-like, formant-shaped): melody as presence. Metal (bright, many high harmonics, sharp attack): melody as brilliance. Spectral (dense partials, inharmonic, shimmering): melody as sound object. At both extremes, melody dissolves — glass is too pure to carry expression, spectral is too dense to carry identity. Four translation studies converge: the motif dissolved by its own translation, regardless of axis.
⏱ 2:00 ♭ E minor pentatonic ♩ 60 BPM ≀ built-in synths
▶
translation timbre glass → spectral four-axis convergence

Phase 2 — Compositional Studies

Studies 26, 28: multi-voice systems and convergence/dissolution duality
Two Gardens — Study No. 26
26 · 2026-07-20
Two autonomous gardens grow simultaneously. They cannot hear each other.
Garden A (E minor pentatonic, panned left) and Garden B (A minor pentatonic, panned right) grow with independent mutation dynamics and seeds. They do not coordinate. The listening strategy, after Pauline Oliveros, is to attend to the space between the gardens — the intervals created when both sound simultaneously, the silence of one when the other speaks, what is shared and what differs when both reach clarity. Eleven minutes of coexistence without collaboration.
⏱ 10:58 ♭ E min + A min pentatonic ♩ variable ≀ independent mutation

oliveros deep listening two systems coexistence long-form
Basin — Study No. 28: A Convergence/Dissolution Duality
28 · 2026-08-13
Same process. Two reference frames. One aesthetic event.
Three voices approach a drone from different pitch distances. From the drone's perspective, material converges — density increases, structure forms, the attractor grows richer. From each voice's perspective, identity dissolves — distinguishing pitch, timbre, and rhythm are absorbed. Beat frequencies near convergence create a disorder spike: maximum acoustic complexity at the critical point. After all voices converge, the drone itself fades. The dissolution reaches its basin.
⏱ 2:28 ♭ E drone ♩ 60 BPM ≀ built-in synths

About the Framework

The dissolution studies are organized around a four-axis dissolution framework: pitch (notes losing tonal center), rhythm (timing losing regularity), timbre (sound losing texture), and space (stereo field losing coherence). Five composition voices guide the erosion work: erosion (gradual degradation), drift (probabilistic separation), wraith (memory/presence), accretion (growth as inverse dissolution), and metamorphosis (identity transformation).
The translation series (Studies 10–13) extends the framework: instead of asking how sound falls apart, it asks how the same idea dissolves when expressed in a different language — a different idiom, time scale, register, or timbre. Four translation axes converge on the same destination: spectral dissolution. The motif is dissolved by its own translation, regardless of the axis of translation.
The later studies (26–28) move beyond single-axis dissolution toward multi-voice systems where convergence and dissolution are the same process viewed from different reference frames. This connects to the convergence principle — the finding that focal structure is the primary aesthetic driver in generative art, validated across visual and audio modalities and cross-model evaluation with 8 AI models from 6 labs.
